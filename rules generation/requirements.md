There are several modes, and only one is applied for one position of the chip on the line. The spatial distance between consecutive mode transitions is strictly positive to prevent infinitely frequent switching.

A generated text provides rules to append new elements to the right of the chip. These rules are parameterized by the chip's current position and its active mode. They can do mathematically continuous or discrete edits to the line.

This text is generated by a machine designed to solve optimization problems. Because it cannot perfectly solve the optimization in a single pass for every scenario, it dynamically generates a new text when it decides to. It decides when it is relevant to generate a new text and how much time does it allow itself for a run, depending on its available CPU resources defined by the user.

When a new text is generated, all the edits made by the previous text are reversed, except what is on the left side of the chip is  to the right of the chip are discarded (using the code in the "reverse logic" folder), and the rules of the new text are applied.
